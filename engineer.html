About my engineering

Me as an engineer

The strengths, perspectives, and experiences I bring to engineering teams — and the areas I am continuing to develop.

What I bring to a team

Problem solving

I enjoy breaking complex engineering problems into smaller, manageable problems and using analysis to work toward practical solutions.

Technical thinking

My experience spans CAD, FEA, mechanical analysis, programming, engineering documentation, and industrial project work.

Adaptability

I have worked across academic projects, engineering design teams, and industry, adapting to different technical problems and team environments.

Collaboration

I value technical discussion and design review, and I am comfortable incorporating different perspectives into an engineering solution.

Strengths

Each strength below is paired with where it showed up and what it does for the team around me.

Technical problem solving

Where it showed up. In MECH 323 I worked through the fatigue analysis one critical location at a time — endurance limits, stress concentration factors, then a DE-Gerber safety factor at every keyway, shoulder, flange and retaining ring groove across three shafts.

What it does for a team. Large analytical problems become a defined list of smaller checks that other people can follow, review, and build on.

Attention to detail

Where it showed up. On the Trans-Northern obsolescence project I consolidated field-collected equipment data — asset ages, nameplate information, technical documentation — into a central database and resolved discrepancies directly with the client's engineering team.

What it does for a team. Downstream decisions are made on data that has already been reconciled, rather than data that has to be questioned later.

Adaptability

Where it showed up. In the same academic year I worked on thermal systems for an EV conversion, vehicle dynamics for an off-road race car, and then moved into a 16-month industry placement across two very different client projects.

What it does for a team. I can pick up unfamiliar tools, drawings and standards quickly and start contributing without a long ramp-up.

Ownership

Where it showed up. In MECH 323 I took responsibility for the calculation set and the tables used to present the results. In APSC 200 I owned the final design introduction, the conclusions and recommendations, and the analysis of how the mouthguard could be adapted across different sports.

What it does for a team. Teammates know a section is genuinely finished — checked, formatted and ready to be built on — rather than handed over half-done.

Growing as an engineer

Areas I am actively working on as I move from student projects toward professional engineering work.

Delegation

As I gain experience working on larger engineering projects, I want to become more effective at delegating technical responsibilities and trusting team members with ownership of their work.

Technical communication

I want to continue improving my ability to communicate complex technical information clearly to both technical and non-technical audiences.

Broader industry experience

I want to continue gaining exposure to different engineering environments so I can better understand where my interests and strengths are best suited.
